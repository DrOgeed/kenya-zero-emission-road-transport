# 1. Methodology

## 1.1 Purpose and question

KEZERT asks one question: **can Kenya phase out fossil-fuelled road vehicles by 2050 without crippling its power sector and national grid, and what does that depend on?** "Crippling" is made measurable through four adequacy indicators that the model reports every year: system peak demand, firm capacity against a 25 % reserve margin, the firm-capacity shortfall, and unserved energy. These are reported alongside total system cost, emissions, fuel imports and fuel-tax revenue.

## 1.2 Modelling framework

The model is written for **OSeMOSYS** (Howells et al., 2011) in the formulation shipped with **MUIO v5.7**, the UN DESA user interface. The unmodified MUIO model file is vendored as `model/osemosys_muio_v5.7.txt`. Four features of that formulation are used deliberately:

| MUIO v5.7 feature | How KEZERT uses it |
|---|---|
| Commodity **balance levels** (timeslice / seasonal / annual) | Electricity is balanced every timeslice. EV charging commodities are balanced **per season**. Because each season is a representative day, this means charging energy only has to match driving energy over the day, so charging can move within the day (smart charging). Fuels and transport services are balanced annually, which keeps the model small. |
| **OperationalProfile** | Fixes the within-day shape of charging for the *unmanaged* and *time-of-use* regimes. |
| **DiscountRateIdv** (technology-specific cost of capital) | Represents the high cost of finance faced by boda boda riders and matatu owners, and the effect of green finance (S10). |
| **User-defined constraints (UDC)** | Implement the reserve margin, VRE integration limits, ZEV diffusion limits, build-rate limits and the V2G resource. MUIO v5.7 has no native reserve-margin equation. |

The LP matrix is generated by `glpsol --check --wlp` from the MUIO model file and solved with **HiGHS** (interior point with crossover). For a 2024–2060 run, a scenario is about 176,000 variables by 248,000 constraints.

## 1.3 Spatial, temporal and sectoral scope

* **Region:** Kenya, one node. Cross-border trade enters as import technologies.
* **Years:** 2024 (calibration base year) to 2060. Results are reported to 2050; 2051–2060 limits end-of-horizon distortions.
* **Timeslices:** 48, inherited from the CCG Kenya PSM: 4 seasons × 2 day types × 6 daily brackets.
  * Seasons inferred from the PSM `YearSplit` (90, 61, 122 and 92 days): Jan–Mar, Apr–May, Jun–Sep, Oct–Dec.
  * Day types: weekday and weekend.
  * Daily brackets: 00–06, 06–09, 09–15, 15–19, 19–21, 21–24 h, inferred from the bracket lengths and solar capacity factors.
  * The 21:00–06:00 brackets approximate EPRA's 22:00–06:00 e-mobility off-peak window.
* **Sectors:**
  * **Power:** the full CCG Kenya Power System Model v3.0.0.
  * **Road transport:** new module.
  * **Rail:** freight and passenger, used as the modal-shift recipient.
  * **Electrolytic hydrogen:** for fuel-cell vehicles.
  * Other petroleum uses (industry, aviation, cooking fuels) are outside scope. Non-transport electricity demand is exogenous.

## 1.5 Power sector: CCG Kenya PSM v3 adapted to MUIO v5.7

The power sector is the published **OSeMOSYS Kenya Power System Model v3.0.0** (Climate Compatible Growth, March 2026; Kihara et al., 2024; Millot et al., 2024). It is ingested programmatically from its own data file, so there is no manual re-keying. The ingestion uses `glpsol` itself to parse the data (`src/kezer/gmpl_io.py`).

What is inherited unchanged:

* Plant-level and resource-zone technologies, including 11 wind and 2 solar supply regions from the geospatial PSM v2.
* Capacity factors per timeslice, availability, efficiencies, lifetimes, fixed and variable costs.
* Technology-specific WACC.
* Committed builds (`TotalAnnualMinCapacity`) and retirement and resource paths (`TotalAnnualMaxCapacity`).
* Transmission and distribution efficiencies, the electricity demand trajectory (5.6 %/yr to 2050) and its timeslice profile.
* Reserve-margin tags and level.
* The VRE integration rules.

Changes, all documented in code and in `docs/generated/technology_list.md`:

1. **Start year 2024.** PSM starts in 2019. Capacity that PSM forces in during 2019–2023 is carried into 2024 as residual capacity and retired after its operational life. Where PSM's retirement path is shorter than that life, `MaxCapacity` is lifted just enough to hold committed builds; one case, wind zone 11 in 2041, is listed.
2. **Horizon extended to 2060.** 2050 values are held constant, and non-transport demand grows 3.5 %/yr after 2050.
3. **Technology learning.** PSM holds VRE and battery costs roughly flat. Multipliers from NREL ATB 2024 (Moderate) and BNEF 2025 apply:
   * solar −35 % by 2050;
   * wind −20 % by 2050;
   * batteries −45 % by 2050.
4. **Battery storage priced as a 4-hour system.** PSM prices batteries per kW (US$800/kW) and lets energy capacity come free. In MUIO's standard storage block that would let the model buy firm reserve capacity with no energy behind it. Instead, the power block (BESS_TECH) is priced as a 4-hour system: US$300/kW power + 4 h × US$150/kWh = US$900/kW in 2024. Only extra energy beyond that is charged separately (BESS001, US$25/kWh). Battery cost learning follows NREL ATB 2024 and BNEF 2025.
5. **Unserved energy.** The backstop is repriced as unserved energy at a VOLL of US$3.6/kWh.
6. **Reserve margin as two UDCs.** Firm capacity ≥ RM(y) × peak load, checked at two timeslices. RM is 1.05 in 2024–26 and rises linearly to PSM's 1.25 planning criterion by 2030. The starting value reflects Kenya's actual margin: modelled 2024 firm capacity is 2.49 GW, matching KPLC's ~2,495 MW firm capacity, against a 2.30 GW peak. Starting at 1.25 would force an instantaneous, unrealistic build in 2024. The two timeslices are:
   * the timeslice of the non-EV system peak;
   * the 19:00–21:00 bracket of the same season.

   Firm capacity uses PSM's derating tags: geothermal and imports 1.0, hydro 0.8, storage 0.75, bioenergy 0.5, wind 0.1, solar 0. Peak load includes EV charging at that timeslice, derived exactly from the charging profile in force: the profile share at that slice ÷ slice length ÷ T&D efficiency. Under smart charging, charging is blocked in those brackets, so EV peak contribution is zero by construction. A penalised slack (`RSV_SHORTFALL`, US$400/kW-yr) keeps every run feasible and **reports** the shortfall instead of hiding it.
7. **VRE integration limits.** PSM's `DemVsWind/DemVsSol` constraints cap each zone's capacity as proportional to grid demand. They are rewritten for MUIO, which has no `UDCMultiplierDemand`: exogenous demand moves to the right-hand side, and EV, rail and electrolyser electricity count as demand through activity multipliers. Electrification therefore also raises the amount of wind and solar the grid can absorb.

## 1.6 Road transport module

**Demand.** There are nine vehicle classes, each with its own service demand:

* passenger-km: motorcycles, three-wheelers, cars, 14-seat matatus, 25–33-seat minibuses, large buses / BRT;
* tonne-km: light commercial vehicles, medium trucks, heavy trucks.

Rail passenger-km and tonne-km are separate demands. Base-year demand is the calibrated stock × annual km × occupancy (or load). It grows at class-specific rates (`demand_growth.csv`) consistent with UN WPP 2024 population and IMF GDP growth. Modal shift is a scenario input (S1, S8), not an endogenous choice. This keeps the cost-optimiser from making behavioural switches it cannot justify.

**Technologies.** Each class offers incumbent and zero-emission drivetrains, 24 vehicle technologies in total:

| Class | Incumbent | Zero-emission and other options |
|---|---|---|
| Motorcycle | petrol | plug-in BEV, battery-swap BEV |
| Car | petrol, diesel | petrol hybrid, BEV |
| Bus | diesel | BEV, fuel-cell |
| Heavy truck | diesel | BEV, fuel-cell, renewable-diesel engine (S9c only) |
| Other classes (three-wheeler, LCV, matatu, minibus, medium truck) | petrol or diesel | BEV |

* **Capacity = vehicle stock** in thousand vehicles. `CapacityToActivityUnit` is annual km × occupancy, so one unit of capacity is one vehicle-year of service.
* **Energy intensity.** `InputActivityRatio` is final energy per passenger-km or tonne-km. BEVs take charging commodities in fixed proportions; for example, cars draw 75 % home and 25 % fast charging.
* **Emissions.** CO2 is attached to vehicle activity (IPCC 2006 factors), which gives emissions by vehicle class directly.
* **Existing stock** retires linearly over the vehicle life, representing a uniform age distribution of the 2024 fleet.
* **Fuel economy.** ICE fuel economy improves 0.5 %/yr, or 1.5 %/yr for cars and LCVs under the fuel-economy standard in S1.

**Calibration.** The 2024 bottom-up fuel use (stock × km × L/100 km) is forced to equal KNBS 2024 road petrol and diesel. The targets are:

* petrol: 97 % of 1,492.6 kt, which is 62.5 PJ;
* diesel: 84 % of 2,208.4 kt, which is 79.8 PJ.

One annual-km scaling factor is solved for the petrol-dominated classes (×0.96) and one for the diesel-dominated classes (×0.88). Both adjustments are small, which indicates the literature priors on stock, km and L/100 km are mutually consistent. `tests/test_pipeline.py` checks the calibration.

**Diffusion limits.** Without a limit, a cost-minimising LP switches the whole fleet the moment a ZEV becomes cheaper over its life, which does not happen in Kenya's credit-constrained, used-import market. A UDC per class therefore limits the zero-emission share of *new* vehicles. A further UDC caps the hybrid share of new cars at 25 % in 2030, rising to 40 % by 2050, reflecting the lagged Japanese used-import supply. The rules are:

* The reference ceiling rises over time; for example, motorcycles go from 15 % in 2025 to 90 % in 2050, and heavy trucks reach 35 % by 2050.
* Within the ceiling, the model adopts ZEVs only when they are cheaper.
* Ban scenarios raise the ceiling linearly to 100 % by the ban year.

**Bans and the 2050 target.** A ban on new registrations, which covers used imports, sets new ICE capacity to zero from the ban year. The zero-emission target sets fossil vehicle activity to zero from 2050, so anything still on the road is scrapped. Its stranded capital is counted.

**Charging layer.** Four charging technologies convert distribution-level electricity (ELC003) into charging commodities:

| Charging technology | Capex (US$/kW) | Efficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Home | 150 | 0.90 |
| Depot | 300 | 0.93 |
| Battery-swap station, incl. the swap-battery inventory | 1,500 → 700 | 0.90 |
| Public fast / corridor | 600 | 0.93 |

Each charger pays for its own kW, so compressing charging into fewer hours requires more charger capacity. The three charging regimes:

* **Unmanaged:** fixed evening-heavy profiles on arrival.
* **Time-of-use (TOU):** ≥70 % of home and depot energy in the off-peak window, the response to EPRA's KES 8 vs 16 per kWh tariff.
* **Smart:** home, depot and swap charging free within the day but blocked 15:00–21:00. Fast charging is need-driven in every regime.

**V2G** (S5) discharges depot-fleet batteries to the grid. It is bounded by a UDC at 20 % availability of the bidirectional power of BEV buses, minibuses, matatus, LCVs and trucks, and it costs battery wear at US$15/MWh.

**Hydrogen.** A grid-connected electrolyser on the transmission bus supplies hydrogen, including compression and dispensing:

* capex US$1,400/kW in 2024, falling to 500 by 2050;
* efficiency 62 % rising to 70 %.

Hydrogen is balanced annually, so its seasonal storage is implicitly free. This is optimistic for fuel-cell vehicles.

## 1.7 Costs and perspective

* **Perspective.** Costs are in US$ 2024 from a societal or system perspective: fuel at ex-tax import parity plus distribution margin, vehicles at economic cost. Taxes are transfers and are reported separately as fuel-tax revenue at risk.
* **Discounting.** Social discount rate 10 %, with 6 % and 14 % as sensitivities. Investment is annualised at technology-specific WACC (`DiscountRateIdv`):

  | Technology group | WACC |
  |---|---|
  | Boda bodas | 25 % (hire purchase / "work-and-pay") |
  | Tuk-tuks | 20 % |
  | Matatus and minibuses | 18 % (SACCO credit) |
  | Cars, LCVs, trucks, buses | 14 % |
  | Power plants | PSM values, 9–13 % |

* **Salvage.** Salvage value at the end of the horizon follows MUIO's linear depreciation.

## 1.8 Indicators

Computed by `src/kezer/results.py` from the solution and written to `results/scenarios/<ID>/`:

* **Fleet and sales** by class and drivetrain; ZEV shares.
* **CO2** by sector and vehicle class.
* **Fuel:** use, import bill, and fuel-tax revenue (≈ US$0.58/L petrol, US$0.50/L diesel).
* **Electricity:** generation and capacity mix; demand split into non-transport, EV charging by charger type, hydrogen and rail.
* **Adequacy:**
  * system peak, reconstructed per timeslice from exogenous demand plus endogenous charging, at generation level;
  * EV load at the peak;
  * firm capacity and reserve margin;
  * firm shortfall;
  * unserved energy.
* **Infrastructure:** grid, charger and storage capacity.
* **Cost:** investment by sector, operating costs, total discounted system cost.
* **Marginal cost of electricity:** from the duals of the distribution-level balance, undiscounted, average and by peak or off-peak bracket.

## 1.9 Verification

1. **MUIO parity.** `scripts/export_muio.py --verify` writes the native MUIO case, runs **MUIO's own** `generateDatafile` and `preprocessData` (imported from the MUIO v5.7 source), and solves the resulting data file. The objective matches the Python pipeline to within 2 × 10⁻⁸ (relative), with identical matrix dimensions. This holds for scenarios that use MUIO scenario layers. CI repeats this on every push.
2. **Calibration test.** Bottom-up 2024 road fuel use reproduces the KNBS figures.
3. **Structural validation.** Every parameter references declared sets, and every technology has an input or output.
4. **Objective reconstruction.** The LP objective omits the constant fixed cost of residual capacity (a known `glpsol --wlp` behaviour). `results.py` adds it back. This was checked against a direct `glpsol` solve.
